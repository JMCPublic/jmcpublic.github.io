twominute.games

About

Two Minute Games is a small, independent collection of daily puzzles, made in England. Everyone gets the same puzzles each day, and each one takes about two minutes. There are no accounts and no sign-ups.

Where things come from

  • Aperture objects are drawn for the game.
  • Aperture Landmarks photos come from Wikimedia Commons. Each one is used under its own Creative Commons licence, and the photographer and licence show after every round. The full list is below.
  • “On this day” facts are hand-picked from, or fetched from, Wikipedia’s “On this day”, shared under CC BY-SA 4.0. Each card links to the Wikipedia page for that date.
  • Daily quotes are well-known sayings, proverbs and quotations, each credited to the person or work it comes from.
  • Natural Wonders figures (lengths, areas, heights and borders) come from named public sources, listed inside each game.

Privacy

We keep this as light as we can.

  • No accounts. We never ask for your name or email address.
  • On your own device the games keep your best times, your daily streak and your sound settings. This lives only in your browser’s storage. Clearing your browser data removes it.
  • Anonymous counts. To see how many people play, each game adds one to a shared counter for visits, games finished and new players, plus a count for the country your device’s time zone points to, the hour of day (UK time) and whether it’s a phone, tablet or computer. Nothing in these counts identifies you, and no list of visitors is kept. The counters are stored with Google Firebase.
  • Feedback and bug reports you choose to send include your message, which game and page you were on, your type of device and browser, and (in Aperture) where you were in the game. They don’t include your name or contact details unless you type them in. Only Two Minute Games can read them.
  • Where visitors come from. If you arrive through a tagged link (for example one posted on LinkedIn or Facebook), we count that link, and the links people share from the games carry a short random code so we can see, anonymously, how far a link travels. We use your device’s time zone to guess your country for a short greeting and our country counts. We never use or store your IP address. If you choose to fill in the “Where in the World” form, we keep only what you type (name and town are optional) and your country, and only Two Minute Games can see it. Ask through the feedback button to have it removed.
  • Advertising. Ads on this site are provided by Google AdSense, which helps keep the games free. Google and its partners use cookies and similar technology to show ads and measure them, and may use what they learn to show ads based on your visits here and to other sites. In the UK, the EU and Switzerland you’ll be asked first, and you can say no or change your mind at any time. See how Google uses information from sites that use its services; you can turn off personalised ads in My Ad Center.
  • Other services. Fonts load from Google Fonts, Landmarks photos from Wikimedia and “On this day” facts from Wikipedia. As with any website, those services see your device’s internet address when your browser fetches from them.

Questions or something wrong? Use the bug button in any game.

Music

The background music is classical music played by orchestras and performers who have released their recordings for anyone to use. The pieces themselves are long out of copyright, and the recordings are public domain or CC0, so no licence or fee is needed. The one exception, Handel's Water Music, is shared under CC BY-SA 3.0, which only asks that we credit it, as we do here. All recordings come from Wikimedia Commons, many of them made by Musopen.
